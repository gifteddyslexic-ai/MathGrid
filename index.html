Counting

Tap any ball to choose a number, then tap ▶ Count. MathGrid counts along the rows, one ball at a time.

Choose 1s, 2s or 5s to count in steps. The step numbers light up in blue, so the pattern shows on the grid.

Rows and Columns count in tens, a whole row or a whole column at a time. A blue divider follows each finished row, and a red one each finished column, just like the times tables.

→ counts forwards. ← starts at the number you chose and counts back to zero.

The equation shows the number reached, and how many tens and ones it has. The Counting quiz asks "How many balls?" and "What comes next?", in seven levels: numbers up to 10; a ten and some more; rows and columns of ten; tens and ones to 50 with 2s and 10s; numbers to 100 with 5s and 10s; counting back; then everything mixed. Every 10 points unlocks the next level.

Lines

Each line is the top row of the 10 × 10 grid; the rows below are shaded until you use Rectangles.

Line of 10: tap a ball to split the line. Blue on one side, red on the other, always 10 altogether. See 3, know 7.

Line of 5: the same with five balls.

Line of 5+: numbers from 6 to 9 are a five and a bit. 7 is 5 and 2, with 3 more to make 10.

Rectangles: tap any ball. A line across and a line down split the whole grid into four rectangles that always make 100. Look at the diagonals: the rectangles opposite each other end in the same digit. For example, 12 and 42 both end in 2, and 18 and 28 both end in 8.

Hide: Bigger side covers the larger part of a line, or the largest rectangle, so the child works it out from what they can see. The Quick look quiz shows everything for a moment, hides one part, and asks how many are hidden.

11 and 12 times tables

MathGrid keeps to base ten, so 11 and 12 are shown as 10 and a bit.

Choose 11 × or 12 ×, then tap a column (or its number along the top). The grid fills 10 rows first, then rings the top 1 or 2 rows again in gold: 12 × 7 = 70 + 14 = 84.

Quiz 11 & 12 asks in two steps at first (10 × 7, then the extra rows). Once a fact has been answered right twice, it asks the whole fact in one go. A fact is secure after 5 right in a row.

Adding & Taking Away Quiz

Switch on Quiz Me, then tap Start Quiz. The first number shows on the grid and four answers appear in the equation.

After you answer, the balls count on (or back) to show why. Sometimes the question asks for the missing number instead, like 38 + ? = 45.

Each right answer adds a point and each wrong one takes one away. Every 10 points is a new level, and the questions get harder: first staying inside a ten, then crossing a ten, then adding or taking tens, then bigger numbers that cross a ten.

Use the + and − switch to choose which kind of question you get. Turn on Voice in Settings to hear the questions read out.

Reset progress?

This sets your adding and taking away score back to 0 and starts again from level 0. Your times tables progress is not affected.

Adding & Taking Away

Tap a ball to choose the first number (blue). Then tap where you want to land.

When adding, tap a ball further on: the red balls are the number added. When taking away, tap a ball further back: those balls get crossed off. The green ball is the answer.

Count: Ones counts on (or back) one ball at a time. Count: Tens counts in jumps. With Jumps: Through ten it jumps to the next ten first, then in tens, then the ones left over. With Jumps: Tens first it keeps the first number whole and adds one ten at a time, straight down the grid, then the ones: 38 + 27 is 38 → 48 → 58, then + 2 = 60 and + 5 = 65. With Jumps: Five and a bit it jumps the tens, then splits the ones into 5 and a bit: 32 − 7 is 32 − 2 = 30, then 30 − 5 = 25.

Tap any number in the equation to hide or show it. Tap the + or − to switch.

Counting

The Count toggle switches between All (taps out every ball one at a time) and Groups (skip-counts a full row at once). Tap whichever side to start it; tap it again to stop.

In Division mode, only Groups is offered — it skip-counts by the divisor, row by row at a quick, fixed pace, marking each completed row with which group it is (1st, 2nd, ...) so you can see how many groups make the quotient. If the fact has a remainder, it then slows down and carries on by ones through the leftover balls to reveal them as a fraction: the remainder as the numerator, and the divisor itself as the denominator once that final row is completed.

How to use MathGrid

Equation Display

Click on numbers to hide/show them.

Tap the × or ÷ symbol itself to switch MathGrid between multiplication and division mode.

Quiz Mode

Switch on Quiz Me, then tap Start Quiz — MathGrid picks a times table at random and starts asking questions right on the grid.

Each question shows four possible answers. A correct pick turns the answer green; a wrong one turns red and shows the right answer before moving on.

The bar fills as you answer facts from that table correctly three times in a row — that's what counts as mastered. Turn on Voice to have questions and feedback read aloud.

You can also drag the red divider yourself to pick a specific table, or tap a ball or a factor in the equation to ask about just that one fact.

Works the same way in Division mode, asking for the dividend, divisor or quotient instead — it's the same facts either way, so mastering one helps the other. Switch on With Remainders there to also get questions that don't divide evenly — answered in three steps: the whole number first, then the remainder, then what it's out of.

What is a Product?

A product is the result you get when you multiply two numbers together.

Example: In 3 × 4 = 12, the number 12 is the product.

On the MathGrid, the product is shown in the main grid cells where the row and column numbers meet.

What are Factors?

Factors are the numbers you multiply together to get a product.

Example: In 3 × 4 = 12, the numbers 3 and 4 are the factors.

On the MathGrid, factors are shown in the row and column headers. When you click a cell, it highlights the factors that make that product.

What is a Quotient?

A quotient is the result you get when you divide one number by another — how many equal groups fit inside it.

Example: In 12 ÷ 4 = 3, the number 3 is the quotient.

On the MathGrid, the quotient is the number of full rows you've counted up to the divisor line, shown highlighted in green.

Background Colour

Pick a background shade for MathGrid. All six are similarly bright, and chosen so none of them clash with the blue, red or green already used for the factors and product.

What is a Remainder?

A remainder is what's left over when a number can't be divided evenly.

Example: In 13 ÷ 4 = 3 R(1), the number 1 is the remainder.

On the MathGrid, the remainder is any leftover balls counted along a final, incomplete row. Tap the remainder in the equation to switch between R(x) and a fraction.
